About the AI

How our AI works, and what it can't do

A plain-English look at how the AI was built and tested, so you know how much to trust a result.

What it does

You give it one photo of a skin spot. It gives back a score for each of 7 common types of skin spots, and the scores add up to 100%. We only name one type when its score is high enough and clearly ahead of the rest. Otherwise we tell you the AI isn't sure.

What it learned from

The AI learned from HAM10000, a free public collection of 10,015 close-up skin photos taken with a skin magnifier (dermatoscope). The photos come from a hospital in Vienna, Austria, and a skin clinic in Queensland, Australia. More than half were confirmed by a lab test (biopsy).

Tschandl P., Rosendahl C., Kittler H. The HAM10000 dataset. Scientific Data 5, 180161 (2018). doi:10.1038/sdata.2018.161 · CC BY-NC 4.0 licence.

How it was built

  • We started from AI models that already understand everyday photos (MobileNetV2 and EfficientNetB0), then taught them about skin spots. We keep whichever did better.
  • Every photo is cropped to a square and resized the same way, both when the AI learns and when it checks your photo.
  • While learning, photos were flipped, turned and lightened or darkened, so the AI copes with different photos.
  • Most photos in the collection are ordinary moles, so we gave the rarer types extra weight to keep the AI fair.
  • Photos of the same spot were never split between learning and testing, so the test results aren't inflated.

Things to keep in mind

  • Close-up photos work best. The AI learned from magnified skin photos. Normal phone photos may give less accurate results.
  • Skin tone matters. Most photos it learned from show lighter skin, so it may be less accurate on darker skin.
  • It only knows 7 types. Other conditions, like rashes or infections, will be matched to the closest of these 7.
  • It is not a medical device. It hasn't been tested in real clinics.

Who it's for

People who want to learn about a skin spot and prepare for a doctor's visit, plus students and researchers. It is for non-commercial use only, and it must never replace a doctor.
